Bevore we go back to the analytical reals, we will further develop the theory of vector bundles. We start by defining properties of maps between bundles: 

\begin{definition}[Mono- and Epimorphisms]
	A bundle morphism $\phi: E \to F$ between two bundles over the same base space is called a \textbf{monomorphism} if all $\phi_x:E_x\to F_{x}$ are monomrophisms. Similar, if all $\phi_x$ are epimorphisms, $\phi$ is called an \textbf{epimorphism}.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Subbundles]
	Let $\pi: E\to X$ be a bundle. A subset $F\subseteq E$ is a \textbf{sub-bundle} of $E$, if the restriction of $\pi$ 
	\begin{equation*}
		\restr{\pi}{F}:F\to X
	\end{equation*} together with the subspace topology and the vectorspace structure from $E$ is a vector bundle.
\end{definition}
\begin{corollary}
	If $F$ is a sub-bundle of $E$, then the inclusion $i:E\to F$ is a monomorphism.
\end{corollary}
\begin{lemma}
	If $\phi:E\to F$ is a monomorphism of bundles, then $\phi(E)$ is a subbundle of $E$ and $\phi: E\to \phi(E)$ is an isomorphism of vecor bundles.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
	 $\phi(E)$ is a sub-bundle since the vectorspace structure of $\phi(E)$ induced from $\phi$ and as a subspace are the same. Hence the local trivializations from $E$ desent to tlocal trivializations of $\phi(E)$. 
\begin{align*}
	\theta: E\oplus G 	&\to 		F\\
	(a,b)				&\mapsto	\phi(a)+i(b)\text{ where $i$ denotes the inclusion of $G$ into $F$.}
\end{align*}	
	This bundle map is fiberwise an isomorphism and hence a bundle isomorphism. 
	To show that $\phi$ is an isomorphism by lemma \ref{thm:vb-isomorphicVectorBundles} it suffices to show that $\phi_x$ is a vectorspace isomorphism in all fibers. 
\end{proof}
