\section{What are Spheres}
In this section we want to inspect representations of homotopical spheres. To give a little overview we will define the sphere to come with an orientation. To be more specific we have the definition:
\begin{definition}[The $k-$-Sphere]
	Let $\R^k$ be the k-dimensional vector space given as the span of $e_1,\dots e_k$ together with the eucliden metric $\norm{\cdot}_2$. Then the set
	\begin{align*}
		S^k\coloneq \{x\in \R^k|\norm{x}_2=1 \}
	\end{align*} is called the \textbf{$k-$sphere}.
\end{definition} This sphere will be our save haven where we will always come back to. In this definition there is no room for "diffrent orientations on a sphere". In our reality we will detect the two  "different orientations" of a space $A$ homotopic to a sphere that arise from two constructions as follows: assume that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the maps $A\to S^k$. then there is an endomorphism $f: S^k\to S^k$ such that our diagramm commutes:

Now we have a lot of spaces that are homotopic or even homeomorph to the sphere. Just to name a view constructions:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item The disc in $\R^k$ modulo its boundary $D^k\slash \partial D^k$.
	\item The cone over the boundary of a $D^k$ disc. 
	\item The resduced and unreduced susopension of a $k-1$ sphere. 
	\item The one-point compactification of a vector space together with an oriented basis.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{corollary} \label{cor: suspension of a sphere is a sphere}
	If we collaps a cone $C(S^k)$ over a shpere at its base, we obtain a sphere. 
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
	We define the homeomorphism as follows:
	\begin{align*}
	\Omega:	C(S^k)\big/ \{0\}\times S^k &\to S^{k+1}\subset \R^{k+2}\\
		\overline{(t,x)} & \mapsto (-\cos(\pi t),\sin(\pi t)x)\,.
	\end{align*} This homeomorphism has the following properties:
If $t>\frac{1}{2}$ we end up in the upper hemissphere. Furthermore, we can view the domain and its image as topological manifolds and if we cut out the collapsed points, we even have a smooth manifold. Here the map is differentiable. 
\end{proof}
We can use this map to construt a new map form $\overline{D^k}\big/ \partial D^k \to S^k $ as follows: 
\begin{definition}\label{def: Disk modulo boundary to sphere}
We can identify $\overline{D^k}\big/ \partial D^k$ with $C(S^{k-1})\big/\{0\}\times S^{k-1} $ as follows:
\begin{align*}
s: \overline{D^k}\big/ \partial D^k & \to C(S^{k-1})\big/\{0\}\times S^{k-1} \\
\overline{x} &\mapsto \overline{(\norm{x},\frac{x}{\norm{x}})}\,.
\end{align*} The composition $\Gamma \coloneq \Omega \circ s$ now gives a map 
\begin{equation*}
	\overline{D^k}\big/ \partial D^k \to  S^{k}
\end{equation*}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma} \label{lem: determined of javobean and collapsing map}
	Let $S^k=B^k\big/\partial B^k$. Let furthermore $D^k\subseteq B^k$ be a closed disk in $B^k$. Let $f: B^k\to D^k$ be the smooth diffeomorphism (that makes $D^k$ a Disk). Let furthermore $A\coloneq \inti(B^k)\setminus f^{-1}(\partial D^k)$. Then $\restr{f}{A}$ is a smooth map between open sets of $\R^k$ and hence we have a well defined jaccobean. We define $\bar{f}:B^k\big/\partial B^k\to D^k\big/ \partial D^k$ to be the quotient map of $f$, and let $g:B^k\big/\partial B^k\to D^k\big/ \partial D^k$ be the collapsing of the komplement of $D^k$. Then 
	\begin{align*}
		(\overline{f}^{-1}\circ g)^*: K^k[S^k]\to K^k[S^k]
	\end{align*} is a group isomorphism and hence $\pm \id$. We have the equivalence of $(f^{-1}\circ g)^*=\id$ if and only if for any $x\in A$:
	\begin{equation*}
		\det(\diff \restr{\overline{f}}{A}\big|_x)>0\, .
	\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

	\begin{equation*}

	\end{equation*} Let $\beta_D$ be a generator of $K^k[D^k\big/\partial D^k]$ (obviously this is isomorphic to $\Z$). Then there are two cases: Either $g^*(\beta_D)=\overline{f}^*(\beta_D)$, or $g^*(\beta_D)=-\overline{f}^*(\beta_D)$. 
	Notice how $\restr{g}{\inti(D)}=\id$ and thereby smooth. Hence the composition $\restr{\overline{f}^{-1}\circ g}{\inti(D)}$ is smooth. Assume that the Homotopie is smooth on $\inti(D)$ (This assumptian can by justified by Lemma 2.6.3 in \cite{Mukherjee2015DifferentialTopology} which tells us that homotopies between maps that are smooth in certain subdomains can be made smooth over said domains.)
